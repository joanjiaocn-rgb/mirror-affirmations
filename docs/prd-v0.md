# PRD v0 - Mirror Affirmations

Date: 2026-07-30
Market: US / English
Product type: Hybrid SEO site + interactive web demo + waitlist
Working brand: Mirror Affirmations

## 1. Decision

Proceed with a validation-first MVP.

The product should not start as a full mobile app. The first build should be a responsive website with a private camera-based web demo and waitlist. The goal is to validate whether users feel enough emotional value from seeing their own face and reading a gentle first-person prompt.

## 2. One-Sentence Positioning

Mirror Affirmations is a private mirror practice that helps you look at yourself and say one gentle affirmation out loud.

## 3. Product Promise

Open the camera, choose a supportive prompt, adjust the text overlay, and practice positive self-talk in a private mirror-like space. The first web version does not record or upload video.

## 4. Why This Can Work

- The core behavior is more embodied than a normal affirmation list: the user sees their own face and speaks to themselves.
- The privacy-first angle is strong: no account, no feed, no cloud upload, and no default recording.
- SEO can support discovery through pages such as "mirror affirmations", "selfie affirmations", "bedtime affirmations", and "positive self talk".
- The web demo can validate demand before investing in native camera recording, local video library, subscriptions, and App Store launch.

## 5. Main Risks

- Camera activation friction: some users may feel awkward or unsafe seeing themselves on screen.
- Wellness compliance: avoid medical, therapy, diagnosis, depression, trauma, or anxiety treatment claims.
- Similar products exist in the affirmation and mirror-work category, so the product must win through privacy, tone, and an unusually gentle first session.
- Retention is uncertain until users return for 3 or more sessions.

## 6. ICP

### Primary ICP

English-speaking women aged 22-35 who already use self-care, journaling, meditation, or affirmation content, but want something more personal and private than reading generic quotes.

Primary use cases:

- Nighttime emotional decompression.
- Work stress after criticism or a hard day.
- Self-doubt before sleep or before leaving home.
- A small self-compassion ritual when journaling feels too heavy.

### Secondary ICP

Students and early-career professionals who want a quick confidence reset before interviews, meetings, classes, or social situations.

### Distribution ICP

Small wellness creators, journaling coaches, yoga teachers, meditation teachers, therapists, and newsletter authors who may link to the free practice as a self-care exercise. They are not the first end users, but they can help distribution.

## 7. User Jobs

- When I feel emotionally tired at night, I want a short private practice so I can calm down without talking to anyone.
- When I am doubting myself, I want to say something kind to my own face so the words feel less abstract.
- When affirmations feel cheesy, I want prompts that sound like something I could actually say.
- When I try the tool, I want to know my camera and face are not being uploaded.

## 8. Phase 0 MVP Scope

Phase 0 validates the first two steps: website SEO plus web demo.

### P0 - Required

- Responsive homepage for the working product.
- SEO pages for major discovery intents.
- Browser camera preview using the front-facing camera when available.
- Mirror preview mode with clear privacy messaging.
- Prompt overlay with adjustable text size.
- Prompt position control: top, center, bottom.
- Prompt library with categories:
  - Bedtime
  - Morning
  - Work stress
  - Low mood
  - Self-doubt
  - Small wins
  - Healing days
- Practice mode only: no video recording in the first web demo.
- Waitlist form with email capture.
- Basic analytics events that do not include photo, video, face, voice, or sensitive text.
- Privacy page explaining local camera behavior and no upload in the demo.

### P1 - After Validation

- Native iOS app MVP.
- Local video recording.
- Automatic playback after recording.
- Local-only video library.
- Calendar streak.
- Vertical private history feed.
- Export to camera roll as an explicit user action.
- Face ID or device lock protection.
- Custom affirmations.

### P2 - Monetization Later

- Premium affirmation packs.
- Guided routines.
- Private reminders.
- Local video archive features.
- Optional one-time purchase or subscription.

Do not add AI emotional summaries until there is a clear privacy, consent, and compliance plan.

## 9. Explicit Not-Do

- No account system.
- No cloud sync.
- No social feed, comments, follows, likes, or public profiles.
- No beauty filters.
- No therapy, diagnosis, or treatment claims.
- No face analysis.
- No emotion detection.
- No AI interpretation of video or facial expression.
- No default video export or sharing prompt.

## 10. Content Tone

The voice should be gentle, direct, and spoken. Avoid motivational slogans.

Good:

- "I can let today be enough."
- "I do not need to fix everything tonight."
- "I am allowed to speak to myself softly."

Avoid:

- "Crush your goals."
- "You are unstoppable."
- "You must love yourself."
- "Defeat anxiety now."

## 11. Competitive Minimum

The first public demo must at least provide:

- Clear private camera experience.
- Readable prompt overlay.
- Real prompts that do not feel like generic quote content.
- Easy category switching.
- Fast mobile performance.
- Credible privacy explanation.
- One clear waitlist CTA.

## 12. Success Metrics

Phase 0 should be judged on behavior, not vanity traffic.

Minimum signals to continue:

- 40 percent or more of demo visitors click "Start private practice".
- 50 percent or more of users who start the demo allow camera permission.
- 20 percent or more of camera-ready users adjust or change a prompt.
- 8 percent or more of demo visitors join the waitlist.
- At least 5 qualitative comments mention "private", "calming", "felt personal", or similar language.

Strong signals:

- 25 percent or more of demo users return within 7 days.
- Users ask for local recording or app reminders without being prompted.
- Wellness creators or coaches are willing to link to the free practice.

Stop or reposition if:

- Most users reject camera permission.
- Users describe the experience as awkward, cringe, or performative.
- Waitlist conversion stays below 3 percent after 300 qualified visits.

## 13. Compliance Guardrails

- Position as self-care, reflection, journaling, and positive self-talk.
- Do not claim to treat anxiety, depression, trauma, or any medical condition.
- Do not collect or transmit video, image, audio, biometric, or face data in the web demo.
- Use explicit camera permission messaging before requesting access.
- Provide a privacy page before launch.
- If AI is added later, require a separate consent screen and privacy review.

Reference checks:

- Apple App Store Review Guidelines: https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/
- Apple Product Page Optimization: https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page-optimization/
- Apple Search Ads: https://ads.apple.com/app-store

## 14. Owner Review Questions

- Confirm working brand: Mirror Affirmations, Selfie Affirmations, or another name.
- Confirm target language for first launch: English only is recommended.
- Confirm whether the first build should include email waitlist through a simple hosted form or a backend endpoint.
- Confirm whether the first demo should avoid recording completely. Recommendation: yes.

## 15. Downstream Handoff

Next stage: SEO copy freeze plus visual design source.

Minimum handoff:

- Product promise: private mirror affirmation practice.
- MVP: website + no-recording web demo + waitlist.
- Primary risk: camera permission and emotional awkwardness.
- Main conversion: waitlist signup after demo use.
- Non-negotiable privacy rule: no video/photo/audio upload in web demo.
